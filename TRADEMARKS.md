# Trademark policy

Required by §15.6. Short on purpose: a policy nobody finishes reading protects
nothing.

The Apache-2.0 licence in `LICENSE` grants you broad rights to the **software**.
It does not grant rights to the **name** or the **logo**. That separation is
deliberate and it is the same one the Linux, Python and Rust projects make, for
the same reason: the name is how a user knows whether the thing they downloaded
is the thing we test, patch and issue security advisories for.

## You do not need permission to

- Use DocUp, at any scale, commercially or otherwise.
- Say your product "works with DocUp", "supports DocUp" or "is built on DocUp".
- Write about DocUp, teach it, review it, or criticise it, using the name
  nominatively.
- Distribute DocUp unmodified, under the name DocUp.
- Fork the code. Always. For any reason. Including disagreement with us.

## You do need to rename

If you distribute a **modified** DocUp, pick your own name. Patched builds under
our name send their bug reports and their security expectations to us, and we
cannot honour either for code we did not write.

- ❌ "DocUp Enterprise", "DocUp Pro", "DocUp Plus", "DocUp Cloud"
- ❌ A logo that is ours with a different colour or wordmark
- ❌ A domain or package name whose leading element is `docup`
- ✅ "Foo Docs, a fork of DocUp" — different name, honest lineage, our name used
  nominatively to credit it

Removing our branding from a fork is not just allowed, it is what we are asking
for. Crediting the origin is encouraged.

## There is no commercial exception to buy

§4.4 pledges that DocUp has no open-core tier, and this policy creates no way
around that pledge: there is no paid licence that would let a vendor sell a
differently-featured product under this name. If that ever changes it changes in
public, with an ADR, not quietly in this file.

## Questions

Open a discussion. If you are asking in good faith whether a name is too close,
the answer is usually "that is fine, and thank you for asking".
